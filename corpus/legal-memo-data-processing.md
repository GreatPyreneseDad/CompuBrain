# Legal Memo - Data Processing Addendum Positions

**Privileged and Confidential. Prepared at the request of Legal for the purpose of providing legal advice. Do not forward outside Legal, Security, or the Account Escalation group.**

## Purpose

This memo sets out the company's opening positions and approved fallbacks for four DPA terms that customers most often negotiate: liability caps, audit rights, subprocessor notice, and breach notification timing. Account teams may offer fallbacks listed here without further review. Anything beyond these fallbacks goes to Legal under the escalation path in the final section.

Ridgeline processes fleet telematics, driver identifiers, and shipment records on behalf of customers. Customers in the EU and UK are subject to GDPR and UK GDPR. Our Lisbon team handles most EU-facing customer contact, so the positions below assume GDPR Article 28 terms apply.

## Liability Caps

**Opening position:** Each party's aggregate liability under the DPA is capped at fees paid or payable under the master agreement in the 12 months before the claim.

**Approved fallback:** A separate data protection super-cap of 24 months of fees, applying only to breaches of DPA obligations and not to general contract claims. Liability for regulatory fines is excluded from both caps where the law does not permit limitation.

**Do not offer:** Uncapped liability for data protection breaches, or any cap below 12 months of fees. A customer demanding either goes to Legal.

Note for reviewers: the super-cap should be drafted so it does not stack with the general cap in a way that doubles recovery for a single incident. Use the "greater of" structure in the standard rider, not "sum of."

## Audit Rights

**Opening position:** Customer may review our current SOC 2 Type II report and completed security questionnaire once per 12 months. No on-site audit.

**Approved fallback:** One audit per 12 months, conducted remotely or on-site, on at least 30 days' written notice, limited to business hours and to Ridgeline systems that process the customer's data. The customer bears its own costs. Ridgeline bears its own costs unless the audit identifies a material non-compliance, in which case Ridgeline covers reasonable third-party auditor fees.

**Conditions:** Auditors must sign our standard confidentiality terms. Customers may not access other tenants' data or internal incident records. Audit scope is limited to the security and privacy controls in Annex II of the DPA.

**Do not offer:** Unlimited audits, audits on less than 30 days' notice (except after a confirmed breach affecting that customer, which Security handles separately), or access to source code.

## Subprocessor Notice

**Opening position:** 30 days' prior written notice of any new subprocessor, with a published list maintained at our trust portal.

**Approved fallback:** 14 days' notice for replacement of an existing subprocessor where the replacement is needed for continuity or security reasons, provided the replacement offers equivalent protections. The customer may object on reasonable data protection grounds within the notice period. If we cannot resolve the objection, the customer may terminate the affected services without penalty. We do not offer prepayment credits or fee adjustments as part of this right.

**Flow-down:** All subprocessors must be bound by written terms no less protective than the DPA. Legal reviews any subprocessor that processes location data outside the EEA.

## Breach Notification Timing

**Opening position:** Notice to the customer without undue delay and in any event within 72 hours after we become aware of a personal data breach affecting customer data.

**Approved fallback:** Initial notice within 48 hours of confirmation that a breach has affected customer data, followed by written updates at least every 24 hours until containment, and a final incident report within 30 days. "Confirmation" means Security has determined that customer data was accessed, acquired, or disclosed. Suspected incidents under investigation are not a trigger.

**Why this matters:** Our 72-hour outer limit is intended to match the controller's obligation under GDPR Article 33 to notify the supervisory authority. Customers who demand notice before confirmation will be unable to meet their own deadlines in some cases, so push back on that framing with Legal before agreeing.

## Escalation

Requests outside these fallbacks, combined requests that move two or more terms at once, and any customer requiring a regulator-specific clause go to the Privacy Counsel queue. Account Executives should not commit to positions in writing until Legal confirms. Legal reviews all approved fallbacks each calendar year; the next review is scheduled for Q1 2026 following the update to Annex II.
