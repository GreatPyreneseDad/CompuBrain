# Performance Calibration Notes: August 2026

**Confidential. People team and calibration panel members only. Do not forward.**

## Purpose and scope

This document records the outcome of the August 2026 calibration cycle. It covers the rating distribution by function, promotion counts by level, and three anonymized cases that needed extra discussion. It is for people team members and panel members. It is not for managers to share with their teams, and individual ratings must not be discussed outside the panel.

The rated population was 287 people. We excluded employees with fewer than 90 days in role as of July 31 and anyone who left before calibration closed on August 14.

## Process summary

Managers submitted draft ratings by July 24. Function-level panels met between July 28 and August 7, then a cross-functional panel reviewed the full distribution on August 11 and 12. Ratings were final on August 14. Any rating changed during the cross-functional review was documented with a reason, and the original manager was told before the change was published.

Ratings use a five-point scale: 1 Below expectations, 2 Partially meets, 3 Meets, 4 Exceeds, 5 Outstanding.

Overall, 60 percent of employees rated 3, and 30 percent rated 4 or 5. Only 10 percent rated 1 or 2. Engineering's share of 4s and 5s (30 percent) is close to the company average. Customer Success had the highest share of 2s and below (7 percent), which matches the open coaching plans in that team from Q2. The cross-functional panel changed 14 ratings in total, mostly downward by one point, and all changes are logged in the calibration tracker.

## Three difficult cases

**Case A: Disputed rating (Engineering).** A senior engineer's manager rated them 2. The employee disputed it in writing. The function panel reviewed the goal history and found that two of the three goals had been changed in March without a documented reason. The panel moved the rating to 3 because the original scoring used goals that were no longer the agreed measure. The manager has been asked to log scope changes in the goals tool within five business days going forward.

**Case B: Strong results, open conduct concern (Sales).** An account executive rated 4 on results. Pipeline notes and two peer reports raised concerns about how they treated partner-side contacts. The panel kept the results rating, since the numbers were not in question, but did not promote, and referred the conduct concerns to the HR investigations process. That process is separate. The calibration outcome does not reflect a finding.
