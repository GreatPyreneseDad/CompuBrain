# Acquisition Evaluation: Route Optimization Target (Wayfold)

**Audience:** Executive team only
**Status:** Draft for discussion, not for distribution
**Date:** March 2026

## Summary

Wayfold is a 48-person route optimization company based in Austin, with a product that generates dispatch-ready routes for regional fleets of 20 to 300 trucks. We have discussed a possible acquisition with its founders and board since January. This document sets out strategic fit, the price range under discussion, integration risks, and the diligence questions that remain open. No offer has been made. The recommendation is to proceed to a non-binding letter of intent only if the open items in the final section are resolved to the satisfaction of Finance and Engineering.

## Strategic Fit

Ridgeline shows fleets what happened on the road. Wayfold tells them what should happen next. Their optimization engine ingests the same telematics feeds we already normalize, so the technical overlap is real and the product gap is clear. Our largest churn reason in 2025 was "we can see the problem but dispatch still builds routes by hand," and Wayfold addresses that directly.

Customer overlap is material. Of Wayfold's 37 paying accounts, 14 are also Ridgeline customers, which suggests cross-sell without new logo acquisition. We estimate the combined product could reach mid-market fleets we currently serve only with reporting.

## Price Range Under Discussion

Wayfold's current ARR is approximately $4.1M, growing about 31% year over year, with gross retention near 88%. The range under discussion is $24M to $29M in cash and stock, which is roughly 5.9x to 7.1x current ARR. Corp Dev has not confirmed the stock component. Any structure above $29M would require a separate review by the Finance Committee and would need to be justified by retention data we do not yet have.

## Integration Risks

- **Engineering retention.** Wayfold's optimization core depends on six engineers. Two are the original founders. Without retention packages tied to 24-month vesting, the product degrades quickly after close.
- **Data residency.** Wayfold stores some customer routing data in a single US region without the controls Lisbon requires for EU-based fleet customers. Remediation is estimated at one quarter of platform work.
- **Dependency concentration.** Two telematics partners account for roughly 70% of Wayfold's ingested data. Contract change-of-control clauses have not been reviewed.
- **Product overlap.** Wayfold's dispatch UI and our reporting UI will both claim the same operator persona. We have not decided which surface owns it.

## Open Diligence Questions

1. What are the change-of-control terms in the two telematics agreements, and do either partner have a competing product?
2. What is cohort-level retention for the 23 accounts signed in 2024 and 2025, excluding the two largest?
3. Which of Wayfold's 61 open technical debt items block the EU data work?
4. What is the actual headcount cost of the six core engineers, and what retention level is needed to keep them through 2027?
5. Are there pending IP claims related to the routing algorithm's third-party academic origins?

## Proposed Timeline

- **March 27, 2026:** Decision on whether to issue a non-binding LOI.
- **April 2026:** Technical and legal diligence, including telematics contract review.
- **May 2026:** Financial diligence and final price range confirmation.
- **July 2026:** Target close, if all open questions above are resolved.
